## 1. Request

The request is a Senior unsecured term loan of $500 million, 5-year tenor, for General corporate purposes. This is a hypothetical request used to test the borrower's capacity to carry another $500 million of debt. We assume pricing at 10.00%, the coupon on Kohl's most recent senior issue, the $360 million of senior secured notes due 2030 [10-K Debt note ¶61, p. 54](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Our loan would be unsecured. The revolver is a $1.5 billion senior secured, asset-based facility [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶125, p. 34](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). The 2030 notes are backed by guarantees secured by distribution centers and e-commerce fulfillment centers [10-K Debt note ¶61, p. 54](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). In a downside we would rank behind both [10-K Debt note ¶61, p. 54](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm) [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶125, p. 34](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).

## 2. Borrower overview

Kohl's is an omnichannel retailer. It sells moderately priced proprietary and national brand apparel, footwear, accessories, beauty and home products through stores and a website [10-K Item 7 ¶3, p. 25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). It is a Wisconsin corporation organized in 1988 [10-K Item 1 ¶2, p. 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Total revenue was $15.5 billion in FY2025, down 4.3% after a fall of 7.2% in FY2024. Lower transaction volume drove the drop in net sales [10-K Item 7 ¶52, ¶54, p. 27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Sales fell in every line of business except Accessories, which includes Sephora [10-K Item 7 ¶56, ¶65, p. 27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Digital sales were approximately flat year over year [10-K Item 7 ¶49, p. 26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). The business is seasonal, and a major portion of sales and income comes in the second half of the year [10-K Item 1A ¶37, p. 11](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). For FY2026, management guides net and comparable sales to fall or stay flat [10-K Item 7 ¶32, p. 26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).

## 3. Financial analysis

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Debt / EBITDA | 3.06x | 3.70x | 2.94x |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 2.94x | 3.59x | 2.43x |
| Lease-adj. debt / EBITDAR | 4.25x | 4.93x | 4.16x |
| Interest coverage | 4.26x | 3.69x | 4.60x |
| DSCR | 2.38x | 0.94x | 3.13x |
| Current ratio | 1.31x | 1.08x | 1.46x |
| EBITDA margin | 8.4% | 7.2% | 8.5% |
| Free cash flow | $591 million | $182 million | $1.0 billion |

EBITDA fell 19.8% in FY2024, then rose 12.6% to $1.3 billion in FY2025. One-off items flatter that recovery. Net unusual operating items were a gain of $114 million: the $129 million credit card interchange fee settlement, partly offset by impairment and store-closing costs [10-K Item 7 ¶156, p. 29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Excluding unusual items, EBITDA fell 3.4% to $1.2 billion, compared with $1.5 billion in FY2023. Adjusted operating income was essentially unchanged from the prior year [10-K Item 7 ¶16, p. 25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). That means the improvement in leverage to 2.94x partly rests on a gain that will not recur.

Cash flow from operations rose 113.0% to $1.4 billion, helped by the settlement cash and lower inventory [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶49, p. 32](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). After capex of $372 million, free cash flow was $1.0 billion. Dividends were cut to $56 million from $222 million. Kohl's repaid $730 million and raised $357 million, so total debt declined 10.7% to $3.9 billion [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶73, ¶74, ¶75, p. 33](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Cash rose to $674 million, and the current ratio improved to 1.46x [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶123, p. 34](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).

Leases matter here. Lease liabilities of $2.7 billion bring lease-adjusted debt to $6.6 billion and lease-adjusted leverage to 4.16x. Interest expense eased to $288 million, but that figure is reduced by a $11 million gain on extinguishment from open-market note repurchases [10-K Debt note ¶63, p. 54](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Adding the gain back, interest coverage is 4.43x rather than 4.60x, one band lower on our grade scale; the overall grade is unchanged at 8 [10-K Debt note ¶63, p. 54](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). In FY2024, scheduled principal of $353 million pushed DSCR down to 0.94x, which shows how thin the cushion can get in a weak year.

**Pro forma.** Adding $500 million at 10.00% adds $50 million of interest and brings total debt to $4.4 billion. On a pro forma basis, debt/EBITDA would be 3.31x, net debt/EBITDA 2.80x, lease-adjusted leverage 4.47x, interest coverage 3.92x, DSCR 2.67x and the current ratio 1.46x. The rate comes from a secured issue, so for an unsecured loan this pro forma interest is a lower bound [10-K Debt note ¶61, p. 54](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).

## 4. Strengths

- **Liquidity:** Cash was $674 million, mostly short-term investments [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶5, p. 31](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). The $1.5 billion revolver was undrawn, with $44 million of letters of credit reducing availability [10-K Debt note ¶65, p. 54](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).
- **Near-term maturities:** No senior notes were due within a year at year-end [10-K Debt note ¶50, ¶51, p. 53](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Current debt was $85 million.
- **Cash generation:** FY2025 DSCR was 3.13x and free cash flow was $1.0 billion.
- **Conservative capital allocation:** Buybacks were $0. Management expects to resume them only after overall leverage improves [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶78, p. 34](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).
- **Merchandising:** Gross margin improved on tighter inventory management, merchandise mix and lower shrink [10-K Item 7 ¶96, p. 28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).
- **Rating momentum:** Since the 10-K date, Moody's has moved to B2 positive (last action 2026-04-14) and S&P to B+ stable (last action 2026-06-17).

## 5. Risks and mitigants

- **Subordination:** We would rank behind the secured asset-based revolver and the secured 2030 notes [10-K Debt note ¶61, p. 54](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm) [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶125, p. 34](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). The $1.5 billion revolver could be drawn ahead of us in a downturn. Only partial mitigant: the 2030 notes are just $360 million.
- **Revenue decline:** Revenue fell 4.3% as competition and price comparison intensified [10-K Item 1A ¶32, ¶33, p. 10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Mitigant: lower SG&A spending partly offset the decline [10-K Item 7 ¶101, p. 29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).
- **Earnings quality:** The settlement gain will not recur [10-K Item 7 ¶156, p. 29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). EBITDA excluding unusual items was $1.2 billion.
- **Rating-linked costs:** Downgrades have already raised the coupon on the $425 million of 2031 notes by 175bp in total [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶70, p. 33](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Further S&P downgrades would raise borrowing costs again [10-K Item 1A ¶75, p. 16](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Mitigant: the outlooks have improved, to S&P B+ stable (last action 2026-06-17) and Moody's B2 positive (last action 2026-04-14).
- **Covenants:** The revolver has a springing fixed charge coverage covenant [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶125, p. 34](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). A revolver default could trigger cross-default on other debt [10-K Item 1A ¶76, p. 17](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Mitigant: Kohl's was in compliance with all covenants at year-end [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶125, p. 34](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).
- **Tariffs:** Most goods are made outside the United States, mainly in Asia [10-K Item 1A ¶10, p. 8](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).
- **Credit card income:** Program profitability depends on the health of moderate-income customers and on regulation [10-K Item 1A ¶47, ¶48, p. 12, 13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).

## 6. Stress test

The scenario applies a revenue shock of 20.0% and cuts the EBITDA margin by 300bp, from 8.5% to 5.5%. It also adds 200bp to floating rates. Stressed EBITDA is $687 million. With a floating-rate share of 0.0%, the rate shock adds $0, because long-term debt is fixed and the revolver was undrawn [10-K Item 7A ¶2, ¶3, p. 37](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). Treat that figure as a lower bound: any revolver draw in a downturn would bear SOFR-based interest [10-K Item 7A ¶3, p. 37](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).

Stressed debt/EBITDA is 5.66x and net debt/EBITDA 4.68x. Lease-adjusted leverage is 6.92x, or 6.22x net of cash. Interest coverage is 2.38x, DSCR 0.92x and the current ratio 1.46x.

Two thresholds break:
- **Leverage:** debt/EBITDA of 5.66x is above our leverage limit.
- **DSCR:** 0.92x means cash flow no longer covers debt service.

With the facility added, stressed debt/EBITDA is 6.39x, interest coverage 2.03x and DSCR 0.78x. Extra floating interest stays at $0. Pro forma stressed coverage of 2.03x is just above our coverage threshold. A realistic unsecured rate, above the secured 10.00%, would likely push it below.

## 7. Risk grade

The weighted score of 6.10 maps to a base grade of 6. The component scores are:

- Lease-adjusted leverage: 8 (weakest)
- Interest coverage: 6
- Current ratio: 5
- DSCR: 3 (strongest)

The stress test adds 2 notches, which worsens the final grade to 8 on the 1-to-10 scale. That is consistent with agency ratings of S&P B+ stable (last action 2026-06-17), Moody's B2 positive (last action 2026-04-14) and Fitch BB- negative (last action 2025-04-07).

## 8. Recommendation

I recommend declining the facility as structured, which matches the grade of 8. Base-case metrics are acceptable, with debt/EBITDA of 2.94x and DSCR of 3.13x. However, they depend on a non-recurring settlement gain [10-K Item 7 ¶156, p. 29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm), while underlying EBITDA keeps falling, down 3.4%.

An unsecured lender is structurally behind a large secured revolver and secured notes [10-K Debt note ¶61, p. 54](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm) [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶125, p. 34](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm). That is the wrong place to be for a retailer whose revenue fell 4.3% in FY2025. Under stress, pro forma DSCR is 0.78x, and coverage of 2.03x is likely overstated because the assumed rate is a secured coupon [10-K Debt note ¶61, p. 54](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885639/000119312526115982/kss-20260131.htm).

The improved outlooks, Moody's B2 positive (last action 2026-04-14) and S&P B+ stable (last action 2026-06-17), are encouraging but not yet backed by earnings growth. I would revisit a smaller, secured or ABL-participation structure once EBITDA excluding unusual items stabilises *(analyst judgment)*.

Recommendation: Decline

## 1. Request

DaVita Inc. requests a Senior unsecured term loan of $500 million with a 5-year tenor, for General corporate purposes. The request is hypothetical and tests whether the borrower can carry $500 million of extra debt over 5 years. The facility would be unsecured. The senior secured credit facilities are secured by substantially all assets of DaVita and certain domestic subsidiaries, and they rank ahead of all unsecured debt [10-K Debt note ¶58, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Our loan would therefore rank with the senior notes, which are guaranteed by the same domestic subsidiaries that guarantee the secured facilities [10-K Debt note ¶63, ¶64, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). A 5-year loan would mature after the 2030 maturity wall, alongside Term Loan B-2 [10-K Debt note ¶13, ¶47, p. F-26, F-27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).

## 2. Borrower overview

DaVita is a leading U.S. dialysis provider, and U.S. dialysis is its largest line of business [10-K Item 1 ¶10, p. 3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). U.S. dialysis produced the large majority of consolidated revenue in 2025 [10-K Item 1 ¶52, p. 6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Management estimates it serves more than a third of U.S. dialysis patients [10-K Item 7 ¶59, p. 54](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Government programs, mainly Medicare and Medicare Advantage, pay for most U.S. dialysis revenue [10-K Item 1 ¶53, ¶57, p. 6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Commercial payors pay higher rates and generate nearly all of the company's profits [10-K Item 1 ¶75, p. 7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). The company also has a growing international business, integrated kidney care, and other ancillary services [10-K Item 1 ¶11, ¶12, p. 3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Revenue reached $13.6 billion in FY2025.

## 3. Financial analysis

| Ratio | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Debt / EBITDA | 3.57x | 3.36x | 3.72x |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 3.41x | 3.07x | 3.48x |
| Lease-adj. debt / EBITDAR | 3.66x | 3.44x | 3.72x |
| Interest coverage | 5.89x | 5.98x | 4.76x |
| DSCR | 2.90x | 2.52x | 2.91x |
| Current ratio | 1.19x | 1.26x | 1.29x |
| EBITDA margin | 19.3% | 22.0% | 20.2% |

**Earnings.** Revenue grew 5.6% and then 6.5%. EBITDA rose 19.8% in FY2024 and then fell 2.0% in FY2025, so the margin slipped to 20.2%. FY2024 was helped by a non-cash gain on changes in ownership interests of $109 million [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶116, p. 64](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Excluding that gain, FY2024 EBITDA was $2.7 billion, and FY2025 EBITDA grew 2.0%, so the FY2025 decline disappears on an underlying basis. FY2025 was hurt by charges from the cybersecurity incident [10-K Item 7 ¶111, p. 57](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). U.S. treatment volumes fell in 2025 because of higher mortality and a severe flu season [10-K Item 7 ¶89, p. 56](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Patient care costs per treatment grew faster than revenue per treatment [10-K Item 7 ¶95, ¶108, p. 56, 57](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).

**Leverage and coverage.** Total debt rose 8.7% to $10.3 billion, and debt to EBITDA went up to 3.72x. Interest expense climbed over two years, from $399 million in FY2023 to $470 million in FY2024 and $580 million in FY2025. Management attributes the rise to new senior notes and the expiry of older, cheaper rate caps [10-K Item 7 ¶183, p. 60](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). As a result, interest coverage fell to 4.76x. Debt service coverage rose to 2.91x from 2.52x, mainly because principal due in the next year fell to $109 million from $271 million.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow fell 6.7% to $1.9 billion, and free cash flow was $1.3 billion. The company's own definition of free cash flow also deducts distributions to noncontrolling interests, so it is lower [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶86, p. 64](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Buybacks of $1.8 billion were larger than free cash flow, and net debt rose to $9.6 billion. Buybacks continued after year-end [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶78, p. 63](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Book equity was $1.1 billion in FY2023 and turned negative at -$651 million.

**Liquidity.** Cash was $676 million and the current ratio was 1.29x. Redeemable noncontrolling interests of $1.5 billion carry put provisions that partners can exercise, and $1.4 billion of that sits in the 2026 column of the contractual obligations table, against year-end cash of $676 million [10-K Item 7 ¶352, ¶358, p. 66](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). The mitigant is the $1.5 billion revolving credit facility, which was fully undrawn at year-end [10-K Item 7, Liquidity ¶81, p. 63](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).

**Pro forma for this facility.** Pricing the $500 million loan at 6.75%, the coupon on DaVita's most recent senior unsecured notes, issued in 2025, adds $34 million of annual interest [10-K Debt note ¶63, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). That is above the 5.51% weighted average effective rate on existing debt, because new borrowing costs more than the old average [10-K Debt note ¶114, p. F-30](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). On FY2025 figures, total debt would be $10.8 billion, debt to EBITDA would rise to 3.90x from 3.72x, and interest coverage would ease to 4.50x from 4.76x. That leaves little room below 4.00x, the level above which the credit agreement's restrictions apply, although the agreement measures leverage on its own EBITDA definition [10-K Debt note ¶58, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). The pro forma assumes the proceeds are spent rather than held as cash, EBITDA is unchanged and the loan is repaid at maturity; the grade of 7 stays on reported figures.

## 4. Strengths

- **Market position and predictable revenue.** DaVita has scale and a leading market share [10-K Item 7 ¶59, p. 54](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Medicare provides a relatively predictable, recurring revenue stream [10-K Item 1 ¶65, p. 6](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). CMS finalized a reimbursement increase for 2026 [10-K Item 7 ¶64, p. 55](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).
- **Solid, though declining, cash generation.** Operating cash flow was $1.9 billion, down 6.7% on FY2024, capex was $576 million, and DSCR was 2.91x.
- **Limited unhedged floating-rate exposure.** Only $369 million, or 3.6% of debt, is floating and not covered by interest rate caps [10-K Debt note ¶61, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).
- **Light near-term maturities.** Scheduled maturities are small each year until 2030 [10-K Debt note ¶43, ¶44, ¶45, ¶46, p. F-27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).
- **Stated leverage target.** Management says leverage stayed within its target range throughout 2025 [10-K Item 7 ¶32, p. 52](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).

## 5. Risks and mitigants

- **Commercial payor concentration.** Profits depend on commercial patients, and those rates face downward pressure [10-K Item 1A ¶58, ¶65, p. 28, 29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). *Mitigant:* no single commercial payor accounts for more than a small share of revenue [10-K Item 1 ¶76, p. 8](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).
- **Debt-funded shareholder returns.** The company may take on more debt to fund repurchases [10-K Item 1A ¶164, p. 42](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Equity is now -$651 million. *Mitigant:* the covenant package proposed in the Recommendation section.
- **Earnings shared with partners.** Net income attributable to noncontrolling interests was $332 million in FY2025, earnings that belong to joint-venture partners and reach DaVita's creditors only after distributions to those partners. Our DSCR of 2.91x does not deduct those distributions, so it overstates the cash available to service DaVita's own debt.
- **Structural ranking.** Secured lenders have a claim on substantially all assets ahead of us [10-K Debt note ¶58, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). *Mitigant:* we would require the same subsidiary guarantees as the senior notes [10-K Debt note ¶63, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).
- **Rate caps only pay above their strikes.** The caps pay only when SOFR is at or above the strike [10-K Debt note ¶61, ¶96, p. F-28, F-29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). The weighted average SOFR strike rises from 4.3% in 2026 to 4.5% in 2029, and some caps step up their strikes at end-2026 and end-2027 [10-K Debt note ¶96, ¶97, ¶98, ¶99, ¶100, p. F-29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Below the strike, the company bears rate movements on the hedged debt as well as on the 3.6% that is unhedged.
- **Cap coverage falls in 2029.** Cap notional drops from $3.5 billion in each year from 2025 to 2028 to $1.0 billion in 2029, inside our 5-year tenor [10-K Debt note ¶95, p. F-29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). In the same window the credit agreement's maximum leverage covenant steps down from 5.00x to 4.50x after 2028, so covenant headroom tightens just as hedging thins [10-K Debt note ¶58, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Management says the caps shield exposure through 2029 [10-K Item 7 ¶32, p. 52](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). However, coverage in that year is much thinner, and nothing is hedged after it. *Mitigant:* management's sensitivity shows only a modest net income effect from a parallel rate rise [10-K Item 7A ¶5, p. 69](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).
- **Refinancing concentration.** Most debt matures in 2030 and beyond [10-K Debt note ¶47, ¶48, p. F-27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Term Loan A-2 comes due early if the 2030 notes are still outstanding [10-K Debt note ¶34, p. F-26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).
- **Volume, regulatory and cyber risk.** New drugs that slow kidney disease could reduce demand for dialysis [10-K Item 1A ¶161, p. 42](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Extensive regulation brings legal exposure [10-K Item 1A ¶49, ¶55, p. 26, 27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). The 2025 cyber incident disrupted operations [10-K Item 7 ¶111, p. 57](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).

## 6. Stress test

The scenario applies a revenue shock of 20.0% and a margin shock of 300bp. Under these shocks, the margin falls from 20.2% to 17.2% and EBITDA drops to $1.9 billion. It also adds a rate shock of 200bp, applied only to the unhedged share of debt (3.6%), which adds $7 million of interest. The capped term loans get no extra cost in this scenario, which is too generous: a cap pays only when SOFR is at or above its strike, so capped debt still bears any rise in SOFR up to the strike [10-K Debt note ¶61, ¶96, p. F-28, F-29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). The extra interest of $7 million is therefore a lower bound.

Stressed results:

- Debt to EBITDA: 5.47x
- Net debt to EBITDA: 5.11x
- Lease-adjusted leverage: 4.99x
- Interest coverage: 3.20x
- DSCR: 1.62x
- Current ratio: 1.29x

**Pro forma stress.** With the new loan added, stressed debt to EBITDA would reach 5.73x, stressed interest coverage 3.02x and stressed DSCR 1.54x. Leverage breaks the threshold by a wider margin than the reported 5.47x, while coverage and DSCR stay above their thresholds.

One threshold breaks: debt to EBITDA rises to 5.47x, above our leverage limit. Stressed leverage of 5.47x would probably also exceed the credit agreement's 5.00x maximum leverage covenant, though that agreement defines EBITDA differently [10-K Debt note ¶58, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Coverage and DSCR stay adequate. Under stress the real risk is leverage, while interest coverage of 3.20x still supports cash debt service.

## 7. Risk grade

The internal grade is 7 on the 1-to-10 scale. It starts from a base grade of 6, which comes from a weighted score of 5.70. The stress result then makes it worse by 1 notch, because stressed debt to EBITDA of 5.47x breaks the leverage threshold.

Component scores:

- Lease-adjusted leverage: 7
- Interest coverage: 6
- DSCR: 3
- Current ratio: 5

Leverage, scored 7, is the weakest part of the grade, and DSCR, scored 3, is the strongest.

## 8. Recommendation

I recommend approval, with conditions. The grade of 7 reflects real leverage risk, but the cash-flow base is durable. The company generated $1.9 billion of operating cash flow, and DSCR stays at 1.62x even under stress.

The main concern is financial policy, not capacity: buybacks of $1.8 billion have pushed equity to -$651 million. Our unsecured loan also ranks behind the secured lenders [10-K Debt note ¶58, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Rate risk is small today but rises late in our tenor as cap coverage thins [10-K Debt note ¶95, ¶96, p. F-29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). The conditions below address each of these points *(analyst judgment)*.

Recommendation: Approve with conditions:
- (a) Proceeds may not fund share repurchases, and the use of the $500 million must be reported.
- (b) The loan must carry the same subsidiary guarantees as the senior notes [10-K Debt note ¶63, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).
- (c) A maximum debt to EBITDA covenant must be tested quarterly, set with headroom above pro forma leverage of 3.90x and below stressed leverage of 5.47x, and no looser than the credit agreement's own covenant, which steps down to 4.50x after 2028 [10-K Debt note ¶58, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). Because the credit agreement calculates leverage on its own definition of EBITDA, our covenant should be defined on reported debt to EBITDA as measured in this memo, not by reference to theirs [10-K Debt note ¶58, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).
- (d) Restricted payments must be limited whenever leverage exceeds 4.00x, the level above which the credit agreement's own restrictions apply [10-K Debt note ¶58, p. F-28](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).
- (e) DaVita must hedge floating-rate exposure through the full 5-year tenor, including 2029 and later [10-K Debt note ¶95, p. F-29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm).
